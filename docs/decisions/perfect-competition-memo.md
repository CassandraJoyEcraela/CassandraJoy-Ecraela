# Perfect Competition — Recommendation Memo

**The plan:** To maximize your profit within your budget and labor
availability, you should plant a mix of 10 tomato beds, 20 carrots, and 30
mesclun. Carrots and mesclun are planted at their full allowed amounts. This
will leave 4 beds unplanted, as it would cost more money to plant additional
tomatoes than you would earn from them.

**The judgment call:** If the farmer can only acquire additional ground for
only one crop, it should be used for carrots first. Adding one more carrot
bed increases profit by about $352, compared to only $246 from an additional
mesclun bed. Since the profit gain from expanding carrot production is
higher, buying or leasing land for carrots provides the greatest return and
should be the first priority.

**What would change your answer:** This recommendation is most sensitive to tomato prices and the risk runs in both directions. Bed 10 (the last one you'd plant) costs about $8,248 against an $8,800 price, a margin of about $551. A tomato price drop of 6.27% would erase that margin and make bed 10 itself unprofitable. Planting bed 11 (which costs about $9,391) would require a 6.7% price increase, not the modest bump this plan assumed. So, the recommendation has more cushion against a price drop than it has room before an 11th bed becomes worth adding.
